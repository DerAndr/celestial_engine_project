# Experiment 001 — SH1106 OLED Display (P1)

Status: READY FOR HARDWARE WIRING

## Goal
Validate I2C communication with the SH1106 1.3-inch 128x64 OLED display on I2C1 (GP2 / GP3), verify I2C bus scanning (address 0x3C), and render system bootscreen and staff telemetry.

## Hardware Required
- Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W (flashed with P0+ baseline)
- SH1106 1.3" 128x64 I2C OLED module (4-pin breakout)
- 400-point Breadboard (30 rows, a-j)
- 4x Dupont jumper wires (Male-to-Male or Female-to-Male depending on whether Pico is seated on breadboard)
- Micro-USB cable to host PC

## Breadboard Layout Strategy
1. **Pico Placement**: Spans rows 1 to 20 with 0.7-inch spacing:
   - Left pins (1 to 20) plug into column **`a`** (leaving holes **`b, c, d, e`** on each row accessible for jumpers).
   - Right pins (40 down to 21) plug into column **`f`** (leaving holes **`g, h, i, j`** on each row accessible for jumpers).
   - Because the center divider is between column `e` and `f`, **`a1` and `f1` are completely isolated and cannot short together**.
2. **OLED Placement**: Plug the 4 pins of the OLED into separate consecutive rows below the Pico (rows 24, 25, 26, 27) in column `a` (or `b`).

## Critical Safety Warning: OLED Pin Ordering
> [!CAUTION]
> Different manufacturers use different pin sequences for 4-pin I2C OLED breakouts!
> Common Variant A: `GND | VCC | SCL | SDA`
> Common Variant B: `VCC | GND | SCL | SDA`
> Reversing VCC and GND will instantly damage the display. ALWAYS verify the exact printed labels on your physical board before applying power.
