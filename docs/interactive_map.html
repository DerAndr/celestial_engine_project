Breadboard Anatomy & Wiring Primer

How electrical connectivity works on your 400-point breadboard (30 vertical rows, columns a–j, power rails).

Velleman VTBB3 / Standard 400-Pin

⚡ 1. Power Rails (Outer Columns '+' and '-')

The long vertical strips on the far left and far right run vertically top to bottom.
• '+' (Red line): Power rail (use for 3.3V or 5V).
• '-' (Blue/Black line): Ground rail (GND / 0V reference).
All 30 holes in that column share one continuous metal clip underneath.

🔗 2. Terminal Strips (Rows 1–30, Columns a–e and f–j)

Inside the main grid, holes connect horizontally in 5-hole groups:
• In row N, holes [a, b, c, d, e] are electrically connected.
• In row N, holes [f, g, h, i, j] are electrically connected.
• Rows are isolated from each other: Row 4 is completely separate from Row 5.

🌉 3. Center Trough & Straddling the Pico 2 W

The coordinates below illustrate one placement; verify the actual assembly before using them.
The trench down the middle physically isolates columns a–e from f–j.
• The Pico 2 W header spacing is 0.7 inches (7 pin intervals).
• When placed across rows 1 to 20:
    – Pico Left Pins (1 to 20) plug into column a (holes b, c, d, e on rows 1–20 connect to those pins!).
    – Pico Right Pins (40 down to 21) plug into column f (holes g, h, i, j on rows 1–20 connect to those pins!).
• Because the divider is between e and f, column a is completely isolated from column f!

🛡️ Golden Rules for Safe Wiring

Rule 1: Always Disconnect USB before plugging in or moving wires.
Rule 2: Never short '+' to '-'.
Rule 3: Keep 5V away from Pico GPIO. The Pico is 3.3V logic only!
Rule 4: Verify OLED Pinout before connecting VCC and GND.
